Example 1: Einstein's Mass-Energy Equivalence (Physics)

Problem: Calculate the energy released when $1\text{ gram}$ ($0.001\text{ kg}$) of matter is completely converted into energy.

Formula: E = m × c² | c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s

Solution Steps:
$E = 0.001 \text{ kg} \times (3 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s})^2$
$E = 0.001 \times 9 \times 10^{16} \text{ Joules}$
Result: $E = 9 \times 10^{13} \text{ Joules}$ (Equivalent to powering thousands of homes for an entire year).

Example 2: Schrödinger Wave Equation Application (Quantum Mechanics)

Problem: Determine the probability density of finding a particle in a 1D Infinite Potential Box.

Formula: Ψ_n(x) = √(2/L) × sin(nπx / L)

Explanation: Particles at quantum scales behave as probability waves. Node points occur where the wavefunction $\Psi = 0$, meaning zero probability of particle existence at those exact coordinates.
